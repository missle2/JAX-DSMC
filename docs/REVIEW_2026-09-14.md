# Forward solver, adjoint, verification, and shape-optimization review

Reviewed 14 September 2026. This evaluates the **working tree**, including its uncommitted and untracked implementation and documentation. The repository HEAD is `5d29958a9d1f1e8fa98b0063d12ef5d27bbd020f`; the local SPARTA checkout is `af66be394ef31c63a76781ae6c5dd7854a3b5d1d`.

**Assessment:** the monatomic Cartesian forward solver has useful and substantial validation. The current AD machinery is not yet a validated general gradient of the expected DSMC objective. In addition to the already documented attribution and long-horizon problems, there are independently reproducible score, inflow, and distributed-diagnostic defects. General forward mesh development is reasonable; reliable shape optimization needs additional mathematical and verification work first.

No production implementation was changed for this review. Reproduction scripts and their outputs are in `scratch/review*20260914*`.

## 1. Forward solve versus SPARTA

The shared core is recognizable: monatomic elastic VSS, the same cross-section prefactor and angular law, mass-weighted center-of-mass scattering, uniformly selected distinct partners within each cell, and transport before collisions. The existing S1–S19 register usefully covers frozen majorants, random-number batching, numerical clamps, sort tables, and the relative scattering-frame guard. Those are mostly already documented differences.

The register is incomplete, and several statements about SPARTA are incorrect:

Important qualifications to the majorant discussion:

1. **The thermal formula is an empirical safety rule, not a deterministic upper bound.** Temperature is a second moment; a fixed multiple of thermal speed does not bound an arbitrary finite particle population. The independent probe with 1,000 equal-mass particles, velocities `+1000`, `-1000`, and 998 zeros, and a hard-sphere rate `q=|v_i-v_j|`, yields `Sigma=516.3978` while a valid pair has `q=2000`: margin **0.2582**. The carried maximum may rescue a particular state, but the thermal floor itself offers no general guarantee. Zero violations in a finite set of beams does not establish one.
2. Violations bias the **forward process too**: clipping acceptance replaces `q` by `min(q,Sigma)` for the prescribed proposal budget. They are not solely a gradient problem. Diagnostics need a retry/reject policy; `run_adaptive()` currently reacts only to attempt-capacity clipping.
3. Matching the *mean* accepted count is not proof that changing `Sigma` preserves the finite-step NTC transition law. With rounded deterministic proposals, the distribution of repeated collisions changes. For a two-particle constant-rate model with mean accepted count one, one certain attempt has zero probability of zero accepted events; two attempts accepted with probability one-half have probability one-quarter of zero accepted events. Both have the same mean. Consequently stopping derivatives through a state-dependent majorant requires a fixed-proposal interpretation or a discretization-error argument, not an exact-invariance assertion for every finite step. Poisson uniformization has different mathematical properties; it should be evaluated separately.

For a closed equal-mass collision cell, a conservative alternative can use its exact peculiar energy: `|v_i-v_j|^2 <= 2 sum_l |v_l-u|^2`. This remains a bound through elastic collisions in that stage. It may be expensive in extra proposals; a useful design is a tighter practical proposal plus explicit validation/replay, tested against the conservative bound on small cases.

## 2. Mathematical differences from the adjoint papers

### What is already documented and remains valid

- P1 uses a frozen laboratory-frame outgoing direction for its Maxwell collision map. The production VSS sampler uses an incoming-relative-velocity frame, so the fixed-randomness Jacobian differs, including at `alpha=1`. Equal forward distributions do not require equal per-key derivatives. The special P1 oracle and the documented frame correction are appropriate.
- P2 uses disjoint pair selection and its own virtual-collision probabilities. Production NTC allows repeated collisions within a timestep. The VSS Jacobian and accept/reject local score structures are useful references, but matching their local recursions is not equivalence of the complete stochastic algorithms.
- P3 applies collisions before movement. Here movement precedes collision, giving a different discrete adjoint ordering. Reverse AD correctly follows the implemented order. That verifies the conditional Jacobian, not all missing event-distribution derivatives.
- `ATTRIBUTION.md` already records substantial local-score bias and growing long-horizon variance. These are **known limitations**, not discoveries of this review. Its newer evidence conflicts with older claims of exact unbiasedness elsewhere.

### A. The production surrogate is not generally the full trajectory likelihood estimator

For a stochastic program with correctly represented random decisions and the usual support assumptions,

`d E[J]/dtheta = E[d_pathwise J/dtheta + J sum_e d log p_e/dtheta]`.

A local reward can replace `J` for event `e` only when discarded reward terms have zero expected product with that event's score. The production update puts collision scores on two particle labels and count scores on the cell's current labels. It does not propagate an event's score to all rewards subsequently influenced by those particles. P2's local terminal-label formula is explicitly present in the supplied paper; reproducing it is not an independent proof that this different finite interacting NTC program has an exact full-trajectory gradient.

The correct review conclusion is therefore neither “AD must be right because it is JAX” nor “the oracle proves the physical gradient.” The oracles check a particular recursion. An independently enumerated finite stochastic system or full likelihood estimator is still needed to verify the gradient target.

For a reference implementation, accumulate **each event log probability once**, globally, preserving earlier events even after a particle dies or its slot is reused. Apply the score to the complete terminal scalar, or to each causal running reward. Do not sum current `logh_acc`: pair and count scores have different multiplicities, as ATTRIBUTION correctly notes. This reference will have high variance, but can establish correctness at small scale before designing lower-variance approximations.

### B. “Has a pathwise route” does not mean “needs no score”

Wall temperature, wall velocity, inlet temperature/velocity, and initial temperature change later velocities and positions. Those change later VSS acceptances, wall events, and cell populations. A direct wall sample can be reparameterized without a **new score for that sample**, while indirect score contributions later in the solve remain necessary.

`examples/couette/gradients.py:105` differentiates final hard-binned profiles without consuming `logh_acc`. It thus omits those score paths and derivatives of bin membership. The statement that these complete gradients carry no score and are unaffected by attribution is too strong. P3's thermal-parameter discussion explicitly includes indirect effects through later adjoint propagation. A flip-free FD confirms the same conditional derivative and cannot resolve this omission.

### C. P3's inflow adjoint is a different method

The supplied P3's inflow section propagates a **scalar distributional adjoint** through the locally linearized collision operator, retains exact forward counts and a subset of states, and evaluates local boundary-source scores. It conditions its reported implementation on fixed nominal injection counts; physical intensity derivatives require a separate contribution.

Here the implementation differentiates recycled particle arrays, scores a stochastic rounding of source counts, and differentiates accepted rejection-sampler proposals. It does not implement that ensemble adjoint, its reconstruction, or its Monte Carlo collision quadrature. Reviving slots correctly removes unrelated former histories, but does not supply the missing ensemble-adjoint terms. The M8 naming should not be read as a replication of P3's inflow method.

### D. Count scores are an extension, not P2's `h1`

P2's `h1=1-dt*mu` is the probability that a particle has no virtual collision in its disjoint-pair protocol; P2 treats `dt`, density and the majorant as constants in that derivation. NTC's `P(n|f)` is a cell-level two-point rounded-count distribution. Equating them in §7.3 is not literally correct. Derivatives in `dt`, `fnum`, volume, gas parameters, and source intensity require a derivation for this program. In particular, the rounded-count distribution changes support at integer `f`, producing an actual failure of the present score implementation, documented below.

### E. Boundary regularization needs a joint sampling law

The first definite error is simple: realized events use sampled `tau`, while the scoring function selects an event using `dt`. Fixing that is necessary, but the full construction still needs review:

- A single `tau` is shared by all spatial components of a particle. Crossings of different faces are correlated. A sum of marginal per-axis log probabilities is generally not the log probability of the joint face history.
- After a bounce, later events depend on the re-emitted velocity and remaining time. The original pre-move three-way score does not describe that history.
- If factorizing a branch probability from the random-time integral, the distribution of `tau` **conditional on that branch** depends on the state. Its derivative must be included, or an equivalent jump/boundary estimator derived. Simply adding a marginal branch score to AD at fixed unconditional `tau` is not a general recipe. This is also a point to audit in the supplied P3 derivation; its displayed branch expectations do not make this conditioning explicit. I have not treated the paper itself as independently validated here.

Use one-particle quadrature benchmarks for position as well as velocity observables before extending this construction to arbitrary normals and multiple surfaces. A forward epsilon-equilibrium test is insufficient.

### F. Hard cell assignment is already a sensitivity omission

The documentation acknowledges missing derivatives when a mesh boundary moves. The same event issue occurs when a parameter moves **particles across a fixed collision-cell boundary**. Uniform pairing conditional on cell membership is not an unconditional position-independent pairing law. `cell_of()` freezes that membership; no score compensates for it. Refining the mesh may give a useful consistent approximation, but that is a convergence claim requiring measurement. General shape changes intensify it through fluid support, cut-cell volume, and event topology.

Finally, DiCE's higher-derivative guarantees apply to a correctly specified stochastic computation graph. They do not repair omitted likelihood factors, parameter-dependent baselines, moving support, or the missing ancestry. The current `magic()` docstring should not imply verified Hessians of the physical objective.

## 3. New defects and verification gaps

The following results come from `scratch/review_20260914.py`, not from the existing scratch investigations. “Critical” means capable of producing a plausible wrong gradient or silently invalid forward state in an exposed path; it does not imply every current example triggers it.

### Critical: wrong realized boundary event is scored

**Location:** `boundary.py:464–470`, called at `boundary.py:551`.

`crossing_score()` computes `x_old + v*dt` to choose `p_lo`, `p_stay`, or `p_hi`. `apply_faces()` moves with sampled `tau`. The returned score is consequently not the likelihood of the event that occurred.

**Probe:** one particle at `x=.8`, `v_x=1`, unit interval, `dt=.1`, `epsilon=.1`, diffuse walls, objective identically one. The surrogate value is **1**, but `dJ/dx=-2.8759997094` instead of **0**. The wrong score is independent of the realized random-time branch, so more seeds do not cure it. The relatively large epsilon makes this probe easy to resolve; the event-selection mismatch exists for any positive epsilon near a wall threshold.

**Missing test:** constant-reward mean-score identity and event-by-event agreement with actual `tau`. Current wall tests check crossing probability frequencies, not the score selected by `apply_faces()`.

### Critical: relaxed accommodation still deposits a hard Bernoulli score

**Location:** `boundary.py:298–307`, consumed by `_bounce_once()`.

For `acc_delta>0`, the output velocity is a deterministic smooth blend conditional on a parameter-independent uniform. Nevertheless `log_p` is still the hard-threshold Bernoulli likelihood. The comments promise that this score is removed; the code does not remove it.

**Probe:** 20,000 samples, `acc=.6`, `delta=.1`: plain AD **-0.85501020885**, fixed-key FD **-0.85501020886**, scored AD **-1.41418216822**. This is an extra erroneous term, not the intended smoothing bias.

**Missing test:** relaxed `apply_faces` followed by the production surrogate. The present test verifies only a plain energy derivative. Remove the accommodation score for the relaxed transition; separately validate convergence back to the physical mixture law.

### Critical: the same-trajectory mean baseline is biased

**Location:** `adjoint.py:109–136`; enabled by default in the shock-adjoint example at `examples/shock_adjoint/__init__.py:370`.

`stop_gradient(mean(phi))` prevents its numerical derivative, but does not make the baseline statistically independent of the event score. In general `E[b S]` is nonzero when `b` depends on that event's outcome.

**Exact enumeration:** one Bernoulli particle, `phi=event`, `p=.3`. The true derivative of `E[phi]` is **1**. The unbaselined surrogate gives **1**; `baseline="mean"` gives **0**. More generally, for a single cell whose particles share the count score, centering all terminal rewards by their own mean cancels that count-score contribution exactly.

**Missing test:** the test named `test_baseline_is_value_neutral_and_changes_no_gradient_in_expectation` checks value neutrality and an algebraic difference between derivatives; it never checks the expectation in its name. Use an independent-seed baseline or a pre-event conditional baseline. Leaving out a particle alone is not sufficient when particles interact.

### Critical: open-population score attribution fails even without later collisions

**Locations:** `inflow.py:298`, `adjoint.py:77–85`.

Only newly created particles receive the count score, but adding a particle changes a normalized live-particle mean through its denominator and the weighting of **existing** particles too.

**Exact enumeration:** one permanently live particle and a second born with probability `p`; both have reward one. `objective()` is one for either population. Scoring only the newborn produces expected gradient **0.5**, rather than **0**. This is separate from long-range collision ancestry.

Use a physically explicit objective: an extensive weighted integral, a fixed normalization, or a ratio of random totals. Score the complete chosen scalar for a population-changing event. Also retain no-injection outcomes when their absence influences existing rewards.

### High: rejection-sampled inlet velocities have incorrect pathwise derivatives

**Location:** `inflow.py:109–142`, `inflow.py:212–220`.

The accepted proposal and the chosen trial depend on the speed ratio `s`. Differentiating the accepted proposal while freezing rejection decisions is not an exact reparameterization of the target flux distribution.

**Probe:** at `s=0`, 100,000 samples, mean of the normalized entering normal speed `beta+s`. Sampled mean **0.886829** agrees with numerical quadrature **0.885946**, but the derivative is **0.295610**, against **0.429542** from quadrature of the very same truncated density: about **31% low**.

The existing sampler tests verify conditional moments only. Use a CDF-based/implicit reparameterization, an appropriate rejection-sampling gradient, or the boundary-source likelihood approach. Inflow temperature and stream gradients cannot currently be advertised as exact merely because their frozen-key FD agrees.

### High: inflow face area ignores suppressed dimensions

**Location:** `inflow.py:180–187`, versus `grid.py:100–105`.

For a 1-D domain with `transverse=.01`, source face area is reported as **1** instead of **.01**. Collision volume and density use the latter measure. The inlet flux is therefore **100 times** the consistent value. Include the suppressed measure in every flux and surface tally, and test equivalent 1-D/2-D/3-D representations with non-unit transverse extents.

### High: inlet slot allocation reserves unused capacity and loses rejection failures

**Location:** `inflow.py:282–305`.

Two independent defects:

- `used += capacity` reserves all candidate slots for a face even when it emits fewer particles. With 40 free slots, two faces each requesting ten, and per-face capacity 60, the probe injects **10**, leaves **30 slots unused**, and reports a shortfall of **10**. The second face was denied available storage. Dynamic-slice start clamping near the end is another reason to replace this allocation strategy with compaction of actual accepted candidates.
- `wanted` already excludes failed velocity-sampler candidates, so they never enter `shortfall`. With 20,000 requested entrants and enough capacity/storage, the probe injects **19,990** and reports essentially **zero shortfall** (`3.6e-12`).

Count requested integer emissions, failed samples, actual free-slot shortage, and inserted particles separately, and check their identity. Existing mass-balance tests use the **actual injected count**, so they can pass despite a source flux deficit.

### High: non-root distributed diagnostics are silently discarded

**Location:** `decomp.py:378–387`.

`out_spec=((...), (P(None, AXIS), P(), P()))` claims that per-device diagnostics and migration counts are replicated. They are local values, and no `psum`/`pmax`/`pmin` occurs. `check_rep=False` suppresses the consistency check.

**Two-device probe:** device 1 needs to send ten particles with buffer capacity two. The returned `overflow_send`, `overflow_recv`, `far_jumps`, and `sent` are all **zero**, while **eight live particles remain in the wrong slab**. Total live count remains 18, equal to the initial count: conservation alone does not catch this invalid state.

Return a device axis or reduce appropriately: sum counts, max capacities, min margins. Checksums need their own defined reduction. Add an asymmetric overflow test on a non-root device and validate ownership after migration. The existing tests exercise non-overflowing, largely symmetric cases.

### High: mixed walls do not follow the first physical event

**Location:** `boundary.py:361–432`, `boundary.py:553–583`.

Diffuse hit-time searches ignore specular/outflow faces, which are handled only after diffuse passes. A specular reflection can enable a subsequent diffuse hit that the search never considered; an earlier outflow can also make a later hypothetical wall irrelevant.

**Probe:** interval `[0,1]`, low wall specular, high wall diffuse, particle `x=.2`, `v_x=-1`, `dt=1.5`. It should hit the low wall at `.2`, the high diffuse wall at `1.2`, then travel with its newly sampled velocity. The implementation returns `x=.7`, `v_x=-1`, and **zero recrossings**, effectively treating the second event as specular.

This intentionally large-step probe exposes an unsupported event sequence. Tiny geometry features make equivalent sequences possible at otherwise acceptable global steps. `n_bounce_passes()` also incorrectly argues that opposite thermal walls cannot both be hit: re-emission from one wall points into the domain, toward the opposite wall. `SimStatic.K_bounce` is not used by this path. Implement earliest-event tracking with a real bounce budget and replay on exhaustion.

### High: rounded-count score fails at integer means

**Location:** `collide/ntc.py:119–126`; analogous source rounding in `inflow.py`.

At integer `f`, only the lower-count outcome has positive probability, but the code differentiates `log(1-frac(f))` as if its support were unchanged. A constant reward passed through `magic(logP)` at `f=1` has gradient **-1**, instead of **0**, for every seed.

For a general reward, the rounded-count expectation is only piecewise smooth at integer means; handling it needs explicit one-sided/weak-derivative conventions or a different proposal law. A finite derivative is not a valid answer by itself. Bernoulli accommodation at endpoints has an analogous zero-probability-outcome issue for one-sided physical derivatives. Include integer, near-integer, zero-rate, and endpoint cases in the stochastic-gradient tests.

### Medium: Couette convergence report uses fourth-power weights

**Location:** `examples/couette/report.py:125–127`.

It sets `w=1/se**2`, then multiplies both the design matrix and observations by `w` before least squares. This minimizes residuals weighted by **`1/se**4`**. Inverse-variance weighted least squares requires multiplying by **`1/se`**. The reported extrapolated intercept and its interpretation need recalculation.

Also, the uncertainty assigned to the cell-size correction is the standard error of the difference of the two finest points, not a propagated fit covariance. Holding `dx/lambda` fixed does not prove that the cell-size bias is independent of Knudsen number: a `Kn*(dx/lambda)^2` interaction is possible. The cell-size sweep changes `dt` simultaneously (`dt ∝ dx`), so it does not independently determine spatial order. These are verification limitations even after correcting the weighting bug.

### Additional smaller gaps

- `emit_moments=False` still returns full `CellMoments`; only the string `"none"` suppresses output. `cell_temperature()` exists but `_step()` calls `moments()` unconditionally. Compiler dead-code elimination may remove unused fields when an outer jitted caller discards them, so do not assume all eager intermediates become GPU allocations. The API behavior and performance claims nevertheless disagree with implementation.
- `run_decomposed()` ignores `emit_moments`, has no per-step rematerialization, no `step0`, no diffuse/inflow interface, and no reward interface. Its existence does not demonstrate a scalable distributed boundary adjoint.
- In Couette and inlet-shock profile drivers, diagnostics are retained from only the last warm-up block; sampling-block diagnostics are discarded. Earlier or later failures can be absent from the final report. Warm-up is rounded down to whole `BLOCK`s while the next RNG step index uses the requested warm-up count.
- The per-face checksum is based on a periodic fold of already processed coordinates, rather than explicit diffuse hit IDs, accommodation decisions, source outcomes, and bounce count. It is not a complete event-history guard for boundary FD checks.
- `MomentAccumulator` returns mean velocity **5** for snapshots containing one particle at velocity 0 and nine particles at velocity 10; pooling the raw particle tallies gives **9**. Both are definable time statistics, but they are not interchangeable, and only the latter matches the claimed SPARTA pooling semantics.
- Fixed per-cell particle weighting, valid physical species, valid capacities and positive volumes need enforced setup contracts. A callable helper that a user may skip is not an invariant.

## 4. What the current tests and examples establish

There is strong value in the pairwise conservation, empty-cell scatter regression, finite-gradient tests, sampler moment checks, P1/P2 oracle algebra, and periodic transport tests. The BKW and Bird shock examples provide independent forward evidence; Couette extends that evidence to wall-driven transport. The distinctions between Euler shock positions and kinetic shock structure are correctly made in the documentation.

The gaps are concentrated at interfaces and in claims about stochastic expectations:

| Existing evidence | What it does not establish | Recommended verification |
|---|---|---|
| Flip-free FD against plain AD | Derivative of an expected observable when decisions change | Small exact stochastic enumeration, quadrature, and paired ensemble FD across step sizes |
| P2 hand-recursion equality | Correct production NTC event law, count scores, or complete ancestry | Full global-likelihood reference on 2–4 particles, repeated interactions, several steps |
| Count-score derivatives are nonzero | Correct magnitude, zero mean of scores, or integer-support behavior | `E[score]=0`, constant rewards, analytically known count responses |
| Collision-invariant gradient test | Full scored invariant gradient | That test explicitly uses `scored=False`; add an expectation test of the scored version |
| Inlet velocity moments and actual mass balance | Correct source intensity or source derivative | Quadrature derivatives; desired/accepted/inserted balance; non-unit face measure |
| Wall equilibrium under epsilon | Correct regularized boundary derivative | One-particle position/velocity quadrature, constant rewards, joint/multiple event histories |
| Zero-overflow multidevice runs | Accurate diagnostics on all devices or correct reverse migration | Force failure on non-root device; check unique ownership and VJP/FD at fixed ownership |
| Couette viscosity smoke test | Percent-level transport accuracy | It allows `0.3 < mu_meas/mu_CE < 6`; retain a separate statistical validation tier with explicit tolerances |
| Couette continuum gradient profiles | Exact finite-Kn targets or converged stationary derivatives | Validate the same finite-time DSMC objective by ensemble FD, then study Kn and horizon limits |

Two reference-comparison qualifications deserve explicit recording:

1. Bird's normal-shock discussion associated with the `6.2` thickness states `omega=.81`, `alpha=1.6625`; the default example uses `alpha=1.40`. The normalization discussion correctly notices those parameters, but a matched reference run is still preferable to treating the published scalar as an exact percent-level target for the current gas. This is a validation qualification, not evidence the shock solver is wrong.
2. The Couette gradient case uses a gap of five mean free paths. First-order Maxwell slip and `dT/dT_wall=1` are continuum-model predictions with additional assumptions, not exact kinetic identities at that Knudsen number. Wall temperature changes transport, density distribution, and temperature-dependent rarefaction in VSS. Agreement with these simplified targets cannot replace finite-time stochastic-gradient validation.

Avoid overgeneralizing ATTRIBUTION's measurements: omitted covariance terms have no universal sign, so a local score estimate is **not a mathematical lower bound on gradient magnitude**. Likewise, an observed flat variance-versus-particle-count range is useful evidence for that protocol, not a theorem that additional particles can never reduce long-horizon variance.

## 5. Acceleration and memory reduction

### First correct the memory model

Wrapping a scan body in `jax.checkpoint` removes many intra-step residuals, but reverse mode still retains the inputs needed to replay successive steps. It does **not** make storage independent of the number of timesteps. This agrees with JAX's distinction between local rematerialization and recursive checkpointing.

Measured with the existing shock-adjoint value-and-gradient entry point, CPU backend, `N=320`, `C=16`, `A_max=128`:

| Timesteps | Compiled temporary bytes |
|---:|---:|
| 4 | 1,313,800 |
| 16 | 1,472,840 |
| 64 | 2,109,128 |
| 256 | 4,654,280 |

The slope is about **13,256 bytes per additional step** for this case. The fit is essentially linear. The documentation's roughly 4,300 bytes per particle was measured at one short horizon and one attempt capacity. Extrapolating it to arbitrarily long trajectories or adding four GPUs' memories without measuring the distributed reverse path is unjustified.

A useful working model is `memory ≈ retained time checkpoints + one replayed step's collision residuals + random draws + outputs`. Both timestep count and attempt capacity matter. Exact constants depend on compiler/backend and differentiated parameters.

### Prioritized opportunities

1. **Checkpoint blocks of timesteps, not only individual steps.** Use nested scans with block checkpointing, retaining block-boundary states and rematerializing inside a block. A simple block size near `sqrt(M)` can trade replay for approximately `O(N sqrt(M))` retained state instead of `O(N M)`; recursive schedules offer further tradeoffs. This preserves the full-horizon derivative. Detaching a warm-up or truncating the gradient does not preserve it and must be called a different estimator.
2. **Reduce collision residual storage with block rematerialization or a custom VJP.** The collision slot scan repeatedly updates large particle arrays. A backwards event replay needs local pair states, pair indices, random scattering parameters, and event likelihood data, not a full copy of every array for each attempted event. Measure actual residuals before choosing a layout. Rejected attempts still require their score derivatives even though their velocity map is identity; do not drop them to save space. There is no safe general inverse of P1's rank-deficient map.
3. **Use forward-mode for very few design variables.** Current wall examples differentiate two scalar parameters. JVPs propagate a few tangent states with bounded temporal storage and may be preferable to reverse mode for those cases. This does not fix missing stochastic terms or chaotic sensitivity, but it can remove the reverse tape. Reverse mode becomes more attractive for many shape controls and few scalar objectives.
4. **Stream seed batches.** Batch enough independent seeds to utilize the device, then accumulate means, second moments and paired FD differences on the host/device. Avoid multiplying the entire AD tape by a large ensemble `vmap`. Use independent/cross-seed baselines; do not buy apparent variance reduction by subtracting a correlated terminal reward.
5. **Reduce padded collision work.** `A_max` is global and every cell executes all slots. Group cells by occupancy/required attempts using a small number of static buckets; track and retry bucket overflow. Preserve the physical collision-cell resolution. Increasing cell size merely to improve GPU utilization can change transport. Adopt stable cell/event keys if bucket reshaping would otherwise scramble common random numbers.
6. **Benchmark a fixed-proposal or Poisson-uniformized formulation.** A fixed attempt count with a properly bounded acceptance probability can move rate sensitivity away from unstable rounded-count scores. The existing fixed-attempt experiment is a useful starting point, but equality of collision means is not a proof of equality of finite-step transition laws. Validate relaxation, transport coefficients, derivative magnitude, timestep convergence, and majorant independence for the chosen process.
7. **Make diagnostics and output explicit modes.** Correct `emit_moments`; use the temperature-only kernel where appropriate; reuse `table.cell`; accumulate only requested observables. Full `CellMoments` has 18 scalar components per cell, not six, so the comment's `C*6*8` byte estimate is also low. Decomposed runs currently always stack full moment histories.
8. **Optimize the hot kernel only after measuring it.** Persistent per-cell kernels, an event-block RNG scheme, or a custom compiled collision kernel could amortize the slot launch overhead while retaining sequential collisions within a cell. Test `prevent_cse=False` for rematerialized scan bodies, donation at outer state boundaries, and selective lower precision for diagnostics/random buffers. These are candidates, not measured speedups. Keep a float64 correctness reference and test gradient statistics before changing state precision.
9. **Bring the distributed path to parity.** First repair reductions and ownership checks; then add checkpointing, source/wall handling, causal objective accumulation, and replay-stable step indices. Validate gradients through migrations. The four-device forward conservation tests are not a capacity or performance demonstration for a four-device adjoint.

Memory and estimator quality are separate constraints. A faster, smaller biased score estimator remains biased; checkpointing cannot cure exploding long-horizon gradient variance. For stationary objectives, assess an ensemble/distributional adjoint, validated truncated-response approximations, or other stationary-response methods against the target before investing in huge trajectory tapes. P3's inflow method is particularly relevant because its backward storage and gradient construction are structurally different.

## 6. Barriers before general meshes and shape optimization

**General forward meshes can be the next development branch. Reliable general shape optimization should not yet be the acceptance claim.** The barriers are concrete:

1. **Correct event tracking.** Introduce a geometry interface for fluid volume, surface area, normals, surface IDs, first ray/surface intersection, cell connectivity and remaining-time tracking. Existing `cell_of/cell_volume/cell_bounds` abstractions are useful but insufficient. Boundary, injection, uniform-position sampling, output, and slab decomposition all contain Cartesian assumptions. Treating a general mesh as a four-function replacement understates the work.
2. **A defined shape derivative.** Decide whether the target is the regularized finite DSMC objective, its expected finite-population value, or a kinetic continuum objective. Shape changes alter collision-cell support, fluid volume, boundary flux, normals, initial particle support, and which surfaces trajectories reach. Pathwise derivatives of intersection positions alone omit changes in hit probability and particle population. Grazing and corner events need explicit treatment. Fixed mesh topology with differentiable geometry is a useful first scope; topology-changing AMR adds another discrete layer.
3. **An unbiased small-system reference.** Repair realized-event scores, relaxation double counting, baselines, birth/death normalization and inlet sampler derivatives. Establish a full likelihood or weak-derivative reference. Then quantify the bias of any practical local approximation versus horizon and geometry. These are prerequisites to interpreting an optimizer's direction.
4. **Surface force and heat-flux objectives.** Implement incident/reflected momentum and energy tallies with correct particle weight, area, elapsed-time and sign conventions. Include explicit geometry dependence and event contributions. Validate wall work and heat/momentum balance. Final gas moments alone are not a replacement for a differentiated drag or heat-transfer objective.
5. **Capacity correctness.** Cut cells create tiny volumes and highly nonuniform occupancies/attempt counts. Guard particle, inlet, migration, collision and bounce capacities over the whole run on all devices. Abort/replay invalid trials before giving an optimizer their objectives and gradients. Uniform `fnum` is a sensible initial restriction; variable weights require actual mass-conserving machinery and corresponding sensitivities.
6. **A useful stationary sensitivity horizon.** Compare equilibration time with the horizon over which gradient signal is resolvable. The existing Couette and shock notes already show that a well-converged forward state may require a trajectory whose derivative has unusable variance. Detaching a converged initial state omits its parameter sensitivity. This issue needs an estimator decision, not only more GPU memory.

A staged acceptance plan:

- **Stage A:** all new small counterexamples become regression tests; constant rewards and conserved quantities have the correct expected gradients; independent baselines and score ownership are documented. Every rank reports failures correctly.
- **Stage B:** one moving planar boundary parameter on a fixed-topology mesh. Start collisionless with analytic/quadrature force and heat flux, then add collisions. Compare AD with paired ensemble FD of the **same objective and horizon**, over perturbation-size and epsilon sweeps.
- **Stage C:** a simple obstacle with fixed connectivity, tested across normal orientations, grazing paths, inlet-edge paths, small cut cells, and multiple hits. Check forward conservation and shape-gradient convergence under mesh, timestep, particle-count and sampling refinements.
- **Stage D:** many shape controls and long-time objectives, after choosing a validated stationary/transient gradient estimator and measuring its full memory/variance budget.

The most valuable next investment is a small independent stochastic-gradient reference plus boundary/source regression coverage. It will prevent a future mesh or optimizer from converting today's hidden estimator errors into convincing but physically wrong designs.
