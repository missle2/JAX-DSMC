**Original assessment.** The monatomic Cartesian forward solver had useful validation; the AD
machinery was not yet a validated gradient of the expected DSMC objective. Reliable shape
optimisation needed more mathematics and verification first. The CPU suite then passed (482
tests); the probes below exposed failures the suite could not see, which is the lasting lesson:
the existing oracles tested particular recursions, not the expected-gradient target.

### Majorant: what the review established (still true)

1. The thermal formula is an empirical safety rule, not an upper bound. With 1000 equal-mass
   particles at `+1000`, `-1000` and 998 zeros the thermal floor is 516.4 while a valid pair has
   `q = 2000` (margin 0.26). Violations bias the **forward** process too (acceptance becomes
   `min(q, Sigma)`), not only the gradient.
2. Matching the mean accepted count does not preserve the finite-step NTC transition law (one
   certain attempt versus two attempts at probability 1/2 have the same mean but different
   zero-event probabilities). Stopping gradients through a state-dependent majorant needs a
   fixed-proposal interpretation or a discretisation-error argument.
3. A conservative closed-form alternative for an equal-mass closed cell is
   `|v_i - v_j|^2 <= 2 sum_l |v_l - u|^2`. This became `ntc.certified_majorant`
   (`SimStatic.majorant_mode="certified"`); measured in ADJOINT_BRANCH_REVIEW sections 4.3 and 4.11.
   `require_valid` enforces zero majorant violations for gradient runs.

## 2. Mathematical points that remain valid

* **A. A local terminal-label surrogate is not a full-likelihood estimator.** The exact score
  contribution is `E[J sum_e grad log p_e]`; dropping `phi_j` terms for particles outside the
  event's attribution set drops exactly `Cov(phi_j, grad l_e)`, non-zero as soon as particles
  interact. A reference estimator must score each event once, globally, with the full scalar
  reward (this became `adjoint.trajectory_surrogate`); pair and count scores have different
  multiplicities, so summing `logh_acc` is wrong ([ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md)). An omitted
  covariance has no universal sign: a local score is not a lower bound on gradient magnitude, and a
  flat variance-versus-N curve is evidence for that protocol, not a theorem.
* **B. "Has a pathwise route" is not "needs no score".** Wall/inlet temperature and velocity change
  later acceptances, wall events and cell membership; the pathwise derivative misses those score
  paths and the derivative of hard bin membership. Frozen-key FD agreement confirms the same
  conditional derivative and cannot reveal the omission. (Examples that differentiate hard-binned
  profiles, such as `examples/couette/gradients.py`, give conditional-derivative checks only.)
* **C. P3's inflow adjoint is a different method** (scalar distributional adjoint, fixed nominal
  counts, retained subset); the production code did not implement it. Resolved by building both the
  P3 transcription (`ensemble_inflow*.py`) and the production route (b).
* **D. Count scores are an extension, not P2's `h1`.** NTC's `P(n|f)` is a cell-level rounded-count
  law. At integer `f` its support changes, so the interior score is not a derivative there (a
  constant reward through `magic(logP)` gave gradient -1 at `f=1`). Route (b) avoids this by using the
  smooth intensity derivative `dN/dc` instead of a count score (ADJOINT_BRANCH_REVIEW 5.4).
* **E. Boundary regularisation needs a joint sampling law.** A single `tau` is shared by all axes;
  later events depend on the re-emitted velocity; the branch-conditional distribution of `tau`
  depends on the state, so scoring a branch while differentiating the unconditional `tau` drops a
  term. This became `wall_adjoint.conditional_time` and was confirmed by one-particle quadrature
  (position derivative 38 % high without it; ADJOINT_BRANCH_REVIEW 4.2).
* **F. Hard cell membership is a sensitivity omission** whenever a parameter moves particles
  across a collision-cell face or a shape moves a cell boundary. The ensemble adjoint handles
  membership by construction; the cut-volume term handles the cell-volume part for shapes.
* DiCE's higher-derivative guarantees apply to a correct stochastic graph only; they do not
  supply omitted likelihood factors, parameter-dependent baselines, or moving support.

| Evidence | Does not establish | Verification that closed it |

Two reference-comparison qualifications still stand: Bird's thickness `6.2` is for `omega = .81`,
`alpha = 1.6625` while the default shock example uses `alpha = 1.40` (use a matched run, do not
treat the number as a percent-level target); and first-order Maxwell slip and `dT/dT_wall = 1` are
continuum-model predictions at the five-mean-free-path Couette gap, not exact kinetic identities.

## 5. Acceleration and memory (measurements that remain useful)

* `jax.checkpoint` of a scan body does not make reverse-mode storage independent of the number of
  steps. Measured compiled temporary bytes for the old shock-adjoint VJP (CPU, `N=320`, `C=16`,
  `A_max=128`): 1.31 MB at 4 steps, 1.47 MB at 16, 2.11 MB at 64, 4.65 MB at 256, i.e. about 13.3 kB
  per extra step. Working model: retained time checkpoints + one replayed step's collision residuals
  + random draws + outputs.
* Block checkpointing (`SimStatic.checkpoint_block_size`) was built from this review. Truncating
  or detaching a warm-up preserves neither the derivative nor the estimator and must be called a
  different estimator.
* Full `CellMoments` has 18 scalar components per cell, not six.
* Memory and estimator quality are separate constraints: a smaller biased estimator is still
  biased, and checkpointing cannot cure long-horizon variance. This is what pointed to the
  ensemble adjoint, whose storage and variance are structurally different. Later memory and speed
  work (record compaction, lane tracker, per-cell backward fit) is recorded in
  ADJOINT_BRANCH_REVIEW 7.10, HANDOFF and `scratch/perf/`; the opportunities list of the original
  review (fixed-proposal/Poisson NTC, bucketing padded collision work, forward-mode for few
  parameters, distributed parity) is otherwise not built.

## 6. Barriers before general meshes and shape optimisation (outcome)

The review's six barriers and A-D acceptance stages were followed in this order; each has an
outcome in ADJOINT_BRANCH_REVIEW:

1. Event tracking and a geometry interface: chronological tracker, then SPARTA-style cut cells
   (`jax_dsmc/mesh/`), forward gated against SPARTA (section 7.1-7.5).
2. A defined shape derivative: the finite-population expected DSMC objective, estimated by the
   ensemble adjoint plus a replayed wall-map VJP (sections 5.11, 6, 7.6).
3. An unbiased small-system reference: exact enumeration, ensemble FD, and the particle-carried
   adjoint (sections 4, 5).
4. Surface force and heat-flux objectives: tallies and windowed rewards (section 7.6).
5. Capacity correctness: diagnostics checked by `require_valid`; capacity failures abort a run.
6. A useful stationary-sensitivity horizon: still open in general; variance grows with horizon for
   every pull-back (sections 4.9, 6); use windowed quasi-steady objectives.

**Lasting recommendation:** every new stochastic estimator needs a small independent reference
(exact enumeration or quadrature) plus a regression test for each boundary/source/count edge
case, before any optimiser relies on it.
