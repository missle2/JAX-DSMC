# `couette` — do the wall boundary conditions carry the right physics?

The M4 gate. Two walls slide in opposite directions and shear the gas between them; the
measured shear viscosity is compared against Chapman–Enskog (Bird 4.64). Nothing in the
solver is told that number, so it is a *target*, not an input — the same standard as the
BKW gate at M1 and the Taylor–Green viscosity gate in [`examples/turbulence`](../turbulence/).

```bash
python3 -m examples.couette --study knudsen   # the gate: Chapman-Enskog as Kn -> 0
python3 -m examples.couette --study gate      # one configuration, 6 seeds
python3 -m examples.couette --study converge  # cell-size study
python3 -m examples.couette --study walls     # every wall treatment side by side
python3 -m examples.couette --study dims      # D = 1, 2, 3
python3 -m examples.couette.report            # tables, figures, RESULTS.md
```

**The gate is `mu_meas/mu_CE` with *both* finite parameters extrapolated away — finite
Knudsen number and finite cell size.** All numbers live in [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md),
which is generated by parsing `results/*.log`. They are deliberately not restated here: an earlier version of this
file hardcoded a viscosity ratio from one run, that run was later shown to be a single
draw of a noisy quantity, and the stale number outlived it. The logs are the source of
truth and the report regenerates from them.

## How the gate is defined, and why it took three tries to define it correctly

`mu = -tau_ns / (du_s/dn)` measured in the bulk, against Bird 4.64 at the bulk
temperature. Argon at 273.15 K, `nrho = 1e21`, `u_wall = ±200 m/s`.

Three properties of the measurement are load-bearing. Each one exists because its absence
produced a clean-looking number that was wrong:

**1. The gate is the `Kn -> 0` intercept, not a single run.** Chapman–Enskog is a
*continuum* result. At finite Knudsen number the Knudsen layers eat into the sheared bulk
and the apparent viscosity falls — measurably, with slope `−0.777 ± 0.143` per unit Kn over
`Kn = 1/10 … 1/30`. Comparing any one finite-Kn run against Chapman–Enskog and reporting
the difference as an error is a category mistake; the sweep and its intercept are the gate.
This is the whole explanation for the offset: **the intercept sits −0.29 % ± 0.95 % from
Chapman–Enskog** while the single-configuration run at `Kn = 1/30` sits −2.5 %.

**2. Every case is an ensemble.** `mu_meas/mu_CE` carries several per cent of Monte Carlo
scatter at any affordable particle count. A single draw of it is not a measurement. Four
successive explanations of an apparent viscosity deficit — under-resolution, cell size,
finite-Kn physics, under-convergence — were each built on single draws before an ensemble
settled it. `--seeds` has a hard minimum of 2 and there is no one-seed mode; every ratio is
reported as mean ± standard error with the seed count beside it.

**Two seeds is enough to prove a quantity scatters and not enough to quote it.** The
2-seed version of the Knudsen sweep put points 5.6σ and 9.2σ off a visually excellent
line, and one of its points read `0.9872 ± 0.0004` where four seeds at the identical
configuration give `0.9779 ± 0.0032` — the tiny SE was two draws happening to land close
together, and it was briefly over-read as evidence. At four seeds the same points sit
within 0.6–2.1σ. Nothing about the physics changed, only whether the error bars meant
anything. Use **four seeds minimum** for anything quoted, and note that
`kn_extrapolation` takes the extrapolation's uncertainty from the fit's residual scatter
rather than from the per-point SEs for exactly this reason; `figure_knudsen`'s second
panel plots the pulls so their reliability is visible rather than assumed.

**3. Warm-up is measured in momentum-diffusion times, never in steps.** Momentum crosses
the gap on `L^2/nu`. A Knudsen sweep necessarily changes `L`, so a constant step budget
runs the widest case for a small fraction of a diffusion time — the first attempt at the
sweep above ran gaps of 15–120 λ at a flat 4 000 steps, where the widest needs 232 105, and
produced a smooth and entirely spurious trend. `diffusion_steps(case)` returns that
timescale in units of `dt` and `--warm-diff` sets warm-up as a multiple of it (default 3).

At the *gate* configuration specifically, warm-up turns out not to be the binding
constraint: going from 1.0 to 3.0 diffusion times moves the ratio by `+0.0041 ± 0.0065`
(`|z| = 0.63`, not significant), which corroborates the earlier measurement that the shear
rate settles by ~0.3 of a diffusion time. So 3.0 is margin rather than necessity here — but
the scaling is what keeps a *sweep* honest, and that is where it is load-bearing.

**4. Cell size is a second, smaller finite parameter — and it is not negligible.** Held at
`Kn = 1/30` and varying only `dx/lambda`, the ratio spans 5.0 % over a 4× range and the two
finest points differ by `+0.0101 ± 0.0039` (`|z| = 2.58`). `O(dx^2)` describes it (residual
`+0.0028` at the coarsest point against `−0.0174` for `O(dx)`) and extrapolates to
**0.9863 as `dx → 0`**, so the gate's own `dx/lambda = 0.30` sits **1.35 % low from
resolution alone**.

This **corrects an earlier reading of this study as flat.** That reading came from single
draws at a constant 4 000-step warm-up — the same two defects as everything else on this
page, and it survived longer because "flat" is exactly what an under-powered study of a
noisy quantity looks like.

Because the Knudsen sweep holds `dx/lambda` fixed at 0.30 for every point, the cell-size
error is a **constant offset on it**: it moves that sweep's intercept and leaves its slope
alone. The two corrections are therefore independent and add, which is the only reason the
combined gate in [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md) is meaningful. Had the Knudsen sweep varied
`dx/lambda` too, the two effects would be confounded and neither extrapolation would mean
anything on its own.

## The wall treatments

Read the shear rate and the slip, **not** the ratio: the specular row divides two numbers
that are both at noise level. Current values in [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md); the
qualitative claims, which are robust and asserted in `tests/test_couette.py`, are:

- **Specular walls drive no shear at all** — two to three orders of magnitude less than
  diffuse, with the gas essentially stationary against a 200 m/s wall. A specular wall
  mirrors the normal component and leaves the tangential ones untouched, so however fast
  it is declared to slide it cannot drag gas. This is the sharpest single discriminator
  between the two wall models.
- **Accommodation interpolates the two, monotonically.** Slip rises as `acc` falls
  1.0 → 0.5 → 0.1 → 0 (specular), and the measured slip tracks Maxwell's
  `u_slip = zeta lambda du/dn` with `zeta = (2-acc)/acc x 1.1466`.
- **The regularisation does not disturb the transport.** [P3]'s `tau ~ N(dt, eps^2)`
  reproduces the deterministic shear rate to within a per cent or so. That is the
  precondition for using it to obtain a thermal-wall adjoint at M7: a smoothing that
  changed the physics would buy a gradient of the wrong problem.

## Geometry, and why it looks transposed

The spatial domain is 1-D and *is* the gap, so the gap direction is spatial axis 0 — which
is velocity component 0. The streamwise direction must therefore be a component that does
**not** advect, i.e. component 1. Getting this backwards asks for a wall sliding along its
own normal, which `WallParams.with_wall_velocity` refuses outright: SPARTA supports it only
through a data-dependent rejection loop (`surf_collide_diffuse.cpp:265-276`), inadmissible
here. That guard caught exactly this mistake the first time this example was written.

In D = 2 and 3 the gap is the *last* axis and the others are periodic, so the same wall
physics is exercised with the periodic directions contributing samples rather than
structure. The `dims` study asserts the three agree to Monte Carlo error.

## Failure counters

`recrossed` and `clipped` are reported for every case and `report.py` **raises** if either
is non-zero rather than plotting the run:

- `clipped` — the NTC `A_max` was undersized, so the collision rate and every transport
  number derived from it are wrong (gap U4's failure mode). This bit a shock case where
  the downstream state is 2.3x denser and attempts scale as the square of the local count.
- `recrossed` — a particle was still outside the domain after the bounce passes. Nonzero
  means the timestep is in the multi-crossing regime and the wall treatment is not being
  applied as intended.

They are summed across the ensemble, never averaged: one clipped attempt anywhere
invalidates the ensemble, and averaging would hide exactly what the counter exists to
catch.

## M7 gradients

[`gradients.py`](gradients.py) validates the boundary derivatives against the closed form:

```bash
python3 -m examples.couette.gradients
```

* `d u_s(y)/d u_wall` against continuum Couette with Maxwell slip -- **worst deviation
  0.068** across the profile, every point within `|z| < 2`.
* `d T(y)/d T_wall` against an analytic **exactly 1** everywhere -- worst deviation 0.066.
  This is the sharp test: Eucken's relation makes the 25.6 K viscous rise independent of
  `T_wall`, so any spurious coupling between wall temperature and transport shows up as a
  tilt.
* `d u_s/d T_wall = 0.038`, which must be ~0.

Both parameters are **pathwise** -- `sample_flux_normal` is reparameterised in `T_wall` and
`vwall` is added after the draw -- so they carry no score and none of the attribution bias
in [`docs/ATTRIBUTION.md`](../../docs/ATTRIBUTION.md) applies. Verified against a fixed-key
finite difference to `3.3e-12` and `2.5e-11`.

**The convergence result is not a `1/sqrt(n)` plot.** Two separate scalings matter and were
confounded in a first attempt:

* **Trajectory length.** The per-seed spread grows `0.037 -> 0.81 -> 341` at 50 -> 200 ->
  800 steps -- a factor of **9 254** over 80x the trajectory. The estimator never becomes
  *wrong*, it becomes uninformative, which looks like success if only the mean is reported.
  `HORIZON = 200` is chosen from that measurement, not by taste.
* **Particle count and ensemble size**, at fixed horizon -- see `gradient_scaling.png`.

## Scope

`tests/test_couette.py` asserts the *structural* claims cheaply (30 s): correct shear sign,
specular drives none, accommodation lowers transfer, regularisation matches deterministic.
The quantitative viscosity gate is a multi-hour run and lives here, matching how the BKW
and Taylor–Green gates are handled. The suite's order-of-magnitude check says explicitly
that it is not the physics gate, so the two cannot be confused.
